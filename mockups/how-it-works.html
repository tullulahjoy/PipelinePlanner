How it works

Follow the live procurement plan, explore alternatives, compare saved pipelines and choose a new baseline when you are ready.

  1. Build a procurement’s plan

    Each procurement has a schedule, meaning its activities and planned dates. Activities are organised into phases, such as writing bids or assessing them. Choose the phases you need and how many weeks each should take.

    Save reusable phases in Library. You can also save combinations of phases as templates to help build future procurements.

  2. Plan around a fixed date

    Choose a date for an important event, such as when a new service must begin. This is the anchor date. The planner uses it to calculate when the surrounding activities need to happen.

  3. Identify problematic overlaps

    An overlap occurs when activities happen at the same time. Some overlaps are acceptable; others may cause problems, for example because they need the same people.

    Overlap rules specify which combinations of phases in different procurements should be flagged. A Warning identifies an undesirable but manageable overlap. A Critical clash identifies an overlap you have defined as unacceptable.

  4. The User Journey

    The current baseline is the 'live' pipeline used for day-to-day planning. To explore changes, choose Edit this pipeline and save your work under a new name. You can keep several alternatives, edit them as often as needed and compare their schedules and overlaps. When ready, confirm your chosen pipeline as the new baseline. It becomes the live plan, and the process can begin again.

How dates are planned

Weeks commence on Mondays

Durations use whole weeks. The timeline uses Mondays as planning points. If an anchor date falls on another day, it is plotted on Monday of that week, while the actual date remains recorded.

Christmas pause

The planner assumes no activity during the two weeks containing Christmas Day and New Year’s Day. These weeks run from Monday to Sunday and do not count towards phase durations.

Six weeks of activity spanning this break therefore occupies eight weeks on the timeline.
